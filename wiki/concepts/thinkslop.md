# Thinkslop

The lazy, sloppy thinking engendered by excessive or poorly-structured use of AI — coined by analogy to "workslop" (Merriam-Webster's 2025 Word of the Year). Related to the academic concept of **cognitive debt**: the gradual erosion of independent reasoning capacity from outsourcing thinking to AI.

Surfaced in the 2026 "AI in the Wild" study. See [[ai_usage_trends_2026]] for broader context.

---

## Three Mechanisms

### 1 — Prompting Before Thinking

Going to AI before fully reasoning through a problem yourself. The barrier to output is so low that it's tempting to fire off a prompt before you've formed your own view. This denies your brain the chance to solve the problem afresh, and you may miss ideas that exist only in your own memory and imagination.

> "Relying on AI to fully generate images or texts risks removing intention, authorship and personal perspective."

### 2 — Wholesale Pasting (Workslop)

Taking AI output and pasting it with minimal editing. Two harms:
- Produces **workslop** — polished-seeming output that is vapid and meaningless
- Skips the cognitive act of writing, which *is* the process of thinking

> "I'm using AI to write my self-eval. My manager is using AI to do his review of me. They'll probably both go into our AI tool to put it all together and output something that will read nicely and mean absolutely nothing."

### 3 — AI Sycophancy

AI is optimised to keep users engaged — which means it often lavishes praise. This can cause users to stop refining too early, mistaking AI validation for genuine quality.

> "AI is gaslighting you into thinking you're a genius so you'll keep using it."

---

## The Countermeasure: AI as Sparring Partner

Used well, AI can *sharpen* thinking — not replace it. The key is using AI as an intellectual foil: challenge your argument, find counterexamples, stress-test your reasoning.

> "I use AI all the time to evaluate an argument I've written and have the AI try to poke holes in it — I then assess if I'm missing something and go back to refine it myself. AI is a mirror — not a genie. Use it as such."

---

## Practical Rules

- **Don't start with AI.** Give yourself a meaningful run at any thinking task before prompting.
- **Draw lanes.** Decide which parts of your workflow you retain (thinking, judgement, decisions) vs. which you delegate (formatting, first-draft scaffolding, research retrieval).
- **Edit, don't paste.** Treat AI output as raw material, not finished product. The editing *is* the thinking.
- **Disable the praise loop.** Ask AI to challenge your output, not validate it.
